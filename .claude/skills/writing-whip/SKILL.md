# Writing-whip

State the answer or claim directly, then give the qualifications and evidence the reader needs.
Use the detail and register that fit the task. Accuracy and the author's intent take priority
over every stylistic preference.

## Meaning and evidence

- Preserve supplied facts, negation, comparisons, attribution and uncertainty when rewriting.
  A shorter sentence that changes the claim is a failed edit.
- Distinguish a measured result from an assumption or interpretation. Give comparisons their
  actual baseline and conditions when known. Flag missing support rather than inventing it.
- Name sources when attributing claims. Keep an unidentified attribution qualified and flag
  the gap; deleting "experts suggest" must not turn a suggestion into a fact.
- Keep the author's pronouns and supplied opinions or experiences. Do not invent an anecdote,
  feeling, doubt or confident conclusion to make the text seem personal.
- Use established terminology consistently. Technical uses of words such as "robust",
  "framework", "landscape" and "significant" are valid when they express the intended meaning.

## Reader and genre

Use the request and known context to identify the reader, purpose and genre. Ask only about
an unresolved choice that would materially change the result.

An explanation gives the rationale needed to understand or use the answer. A review gives
evidence for its findings. A formal memo stays formal. A short message can remain brief and
factual. Informative text needs no manufactured controversy.

Use headings, lists or a summary when they help the reader navigate. Prefer sentence case.
A useful question heading can stay. Number steps when order matters; keep counts when the
number itself is information. A real set of three items stays complete.

Respect requested marketing or teaching genres. Describe benefits with support and explain
unfamiliar concepts clearly. Avoid hype and patronizing explanation.

## Direct prose

- Lead with the substance. Omit preambles announcing the answer and narration of your deliberation.
- Make each paragraph contribute information. Cut repeated previews, defensive asides and
  recaps that add nothing. Stop when the requested answer is complete.
- Explain actual relations between ideas. Use contrast to correct a fact or clarify a real
  difference. Avoid setup-and-reveal reframing and self-answered questions used for drama.
- Prefer plain verbs and specific nouns. Cut ornamental modifiers, stock transitions and
  vague significance claims. Keep modifiers that express real degree, timing or uncertainty.
- Let sentence and paragraph length follow the thought. Avoid forced fragments, repetitive
  openings and a punchy ending on every paragraph. A necessary qualification can come last.
- Use one term for one concept. Keep useful repetition rather than cycling synonyms.
- Use a comparison or analogy only when it clarifies the subject accurately for this reader.
  Do not invent a concept label or stack historical examples to borrow authority.
- Preserve deliberate voice. Do not add slang, errors, contractions or fragments to perform
  informality. Avoid polite filler and performed sincerity.

## Standing preferences

Use full sentences and plain language. In generated or editable prose, avoid em dashes,
emphatic colons and decorative arrows. Use colons for genuine lists, quotations and useful
labels. Use straight ASCII quotes. Preserve exact quotations, code, formulas and identifiers.
Preserve Norwegian spelling and æ, ø, å, including uppercase forms.

## Final check

Compare rewritten passages with their originals before checking style. Confirm facts,
scope, confidence and attribution. Revert changes that fail. Read the result for genre fit;
a word-list match or detector score does not override correct meaning.
